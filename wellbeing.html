UNDERSTANDING WELLBEING

What does it mean
to live well?

Wellbeing is the ongoing experience of being able to live with health, meaning, connection and a sense of balance. It is more than the absence of illness: it is how we care for ourselves, relate to others, meet life's demands and find purpose.

Eight dimensions of wellbeing

01 · BODY

Physical wellbeing

Care for the body through regular movement, nourishing food, sufficient sleep, rest, preventive healthcare and habits that support energy and mobility.

02 · MIND

Mental wellbeing

Support clear thinking, concentration, learning, problem-solving and resilience. Mental wellbeing includes recognising when stress is becoming difficult to manage and seeking appropriate help.

03 · FEELINGS

Emotional wellbeing

Notice and understand emotions, express them constructively, practise self-compassion and develop ways to respond to challenges rather than being overwhelmed by them.

04 · CONNECTION

Social wellbeing

Build supportive relationships with family, friends, colleagues and community. Belonging, trust, communication and mutual care all matter.

05 · MEANING

Spiritual wellbeing

Explore values, purpose, reflection and connection. For some people this is expressed through faith; for others through nature, service, meditation or a sense of meaning.

06 · SECURITY

Financial wellbeing

Develop financial understanding, plan spending, manage commitments, prepare for future needs and seek trustworthy advice when necessary. Financial wellbeing is about confidence and resilience, not wealth alone.

07 · TECHNOLOGY

Digital wellbeing

Use devices and online services intentionally. Create boundaries around notifications and screen time, protect sleep, maintain privacy and make room for offline relationships and activities.

08 · WORK & PURPOSE

Professional wellbeing

Experience meaningful work, fair expectations, supportive leadership, opportunities to learn, psychological safety and a sustainable relationship between work and the rest of life.

WORK-LIFE INTEGRATION

Balance is built through choices.

Work-life balance does not mean every part of life receives equal time every day. It means creating a workable rhythm over time—one that recognises changing responsibilities and protects space for rest, health, family, friendship and personal growth.

  • Set realistic priorities and boundaries.
  • Take restorative breaks and protect time for sleep.
  • Make time for movement, meals and relationships.
  • Review workload and ask for support early.
  • Use technology intentionally instead of constantly.

Mental health matters

Mental health is part of health at every stage of life. It influences how we think, feel, relate, make decisions and respond to stress.

Everyday care

Sleep, movement, connection, time outdoors, manageable routines and mindfulness practices may support general wellbeing.

Recognise pressure

Persistent distress, anxiety, low mood, exhaustion or changes in daily functioning deserve attention and compassionate support.

Reach out

Yoga and meditation can complement wellbeing routines, but they do not replace assessment or treatment from qualified mental-health professionals.

Wellbeing in everyday relationships

At work

Encourage respectful leadership, realistic workloads, inclusion, psychological safety, clear communication, breaks and opportunities for development.

With family

Make time to listen, share responsibilities, communicate openly, respect differences and create rituals that strengthen connection.

With friends

Stay connected, offer practical support, celebrate one another and make space for honest conversations and shared experiences.

How we can work towards wellbeing

Individuals

Build routines around movement, reflection, focus, rest and personal goals.

Families & communities

Support connection, shared learning, inclusion and mutual care.

Organisations

Develop workplace wellbeing initiatives, workshops and healthier ways of working.
